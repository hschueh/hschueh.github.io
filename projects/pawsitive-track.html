Why

Generic fitness trackers treat a dog walk like a jog: distance, pace, calories, done. But anyone walking dogs is really keeping track of the dogs. Did both of them pee? Was that poop okay? Is that the gate with the barking dog behind it? Pawsitive Track is built around those questions. The GPS route is just the map everything else is pinned to.

A walk, start to finish

  1. Pick the pack.

    Start a walk near a familiar spot and the app pre-selects the same dogs you walked there last time.

  2. Walk, and tap as things happen.

    The route draws live on the map. A radial dog menu fans out so you can log pee, poop (with consistency), water, photos or a note for a specific dog. Each log bumps a scoreboard-style +N on the stats panel with a matching haptic, so you can log without looking at the screen.

  3. Pocket the phone.

    Tracking keeps going in the background. On Android you can opt in to showing the live walk over the lock screen, where ending it takes a long press so it can't happen by accident.

  4. Finish and share.

    The walk is saved as a GPX file and synced to Apple Health or Health Connect. You can export it as GPX, or as a photo with the route, stats and a watermark drawn on top, in story, square or landscape ratios.

Features

  • 🗺️ Live GPS tracking

    Real-time route on Google Maps, with pause/resume. It keeps running in the background.

  • 🐕 Per-dog events

    Pee, poop, water, photos and notes, logged per dog and pinned to the map as emoji markers.

  • 📍 Remembered spots

    Notes like “😠 dog behind this gate” or “🗑️ trash can” turn into spots that show up again on later walks.

  • 🐶 Dog profiles

    Per-dog walking stats, a photo gallery from past walks, and sitter notes for whoever is walking them next.

  • 🏆 Achievements

    Streaks, milestones, Early Bird and Night Walker. New badges can be shipped through Remote Config without an app update.

  • 🔥 Walk Recap

    Every walk on one heatmap, plus highlights such as your best streak, favourite stretch and poops picked up. Shareable as a story-sized image.

  • 🛟 Crash recovery

    Detects a walk that was cut off by a crash and offers to resume it on restart.

  • 💾 Backup & restore

    Exports walks, photos and dogs as a single bundle. Restoring merges it into what's already on the device instead of overwriting.

  • 🌗 Polish

    Light/dark/system themes, English and Traditional Chinese, and haptics designed as a deliberate set of patterns.

Under the hood

GPX is the database

There's no backend and no SQLite. A finished walk is written as a GPX file, and the walk history is built by parsing those files. Dog events and notes are stored as named waypoints in the same file. Any GPX tool can open a walk, nothing gets locked in, and backup comes down to bundling files.

GPS fix → locationStore (Zustand) → AsyncStorage # survives a crash mid-walk walk end → GPX file → DocumentDirectory GPX files → parser → history · recap · achievements tracking lifecycle: idle → tracking ⇄ paused → finished → idle

A geohash index for remembered spots

Every camera move asks two questions: which spots are in this viewport, and is there already a spot within 25 m of this new note? A plain filter() over all spots works for a while, then stops scaling. Before choosing, I compared how S2, H3 and geohash are used at Google, Uber and Redis. The app uses a small geohash index: it turns 2-D coordinates into a sortable string, answers a region query with a few range scans, and runs an exact distance check at the end. It needs no native library, which matters on Hermes.

A heatmap that doesn't turn solid red

Routes are split into ~22 m cells. A cell's heat is the number of distinct walks that pass through it, not the number of GPS fixes, so standing still for a long time doesn't outweigh the daily loop. Google's heatmap scales colour to the heaviest single point but paints the sum of overlapping points. That made everything dark red, so the cells are re-binned and the radius re-fitted on every zoom change.

Achievements that survive schema changes

Achievements are computed from aggregates folded over the walk history with a cursor, so unlocking stays cheap no matter how many walks there are. When a new badge needs a new metric (time of day, per-day buckets), a migration re-folds the history so old walks still count. Remote badges use time-window metrics evaluated on the device.

A design system that fails the build

Colour, type and spacing all come from tokens: a forest-green brand ramp, neutrals with a slight green tint, and semantic roles for light and dark. A unit test fails if any component uses a raw hex value, font size or spacing number.

Decision log

Should the map move from Google Maps to OpenStreetMap? I looked into it and decided to stay. Cost was the reason for switching, and it turned out not to apply. The existing react-native-maps setup already did everything the app needed.

Status

Built for my own dogs and used on real walks. Development is active, and crash reporting, analytics and Remote Config are all wired up through Firebase. Next on the roadmap: a pet-friendly places map, mood logging, live-track sharing on the web, and a heads-up alert when you're approaching a spot you marked 😠.
