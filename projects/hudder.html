Side project · experimental prototype

Hudder

Turns an Android phone or tablet into a simple heads-up display mirror for windshield reflection.

  • Kotlin
  • Jetpack Compose
  • MediaProjection
  • Foreground service
  • Material 3

What it is

Put the phone on the dashboard, open a navigation app in one split-screen pane and Hudder in the other, and start capture: Hudder grabs the full display, crops out the opposite pane, and renders it mirrored so its reflection reads correctly on the windshield. Options cover horizontal flip, 180° rotation, a dim backdrop, and which pane to mirror.

How it works

The app tracks its own window bounds and whether Android is in multi-window mode. A foreground service receives frames from a MediaProjection-based capture pipeline, maps the app window into captured-screen coordinates, and crops the opposite split-screen pane. A debug mode can show the raw captured frame with the computed crop rectangle overlaid — split-screen geometry is fiddly, and seeing the math beats guessing at it.

It's an experimental driving-adjacent prototype, not a production automotive system: frame processing is deliberately throttled, and DRM-protected apps capture as black frames.
